## Topics

We welcome contributions on foundational, methodological, and applied problems in probabilistic inference and generative modeling. Relevant topics include, but are not limited to:

1. Sampling, Monte Carlo methods, sequential inference, and variational inference, particularly for high-dimensional, multimodal, or structured distributions.
2. Generative modeling for graphs, 3D structures, time series, text, video, scientific data, and other structured modalities.
3. Scaling and accelerating probabilistic inference and generative models, including amortized inference, learned samplers, inference-time computation, and the role of additional compute in improving inference quality.
4. Uncertainty quantification, calibration, and reliable evaluation of probabilistic models.
5. Interactions between probabilistic inference and foundation models, including language models, vision-language models, world models, and reinforcement learning systems.
6. Probabilistic methods for alignment, control, reward-guided generation, conditional generation, and decision making.
7. Probabilistic inference and generative modeling for scientific applications, including molecular simulation, inverse problems, simulation-based inference, physical systems, and other AI-for-science settings.
8. Applications of probabilistic methods to compression, optimization, planning, decision making, and related problems.
9. Empirical and theoretical studies that expose limitations of current inference or generative modeling paradigms, including careful negative results or new evaluation methodologies.


## Standard Research Track

We welcome ongoing and original research across the workshop's scope that would benefit from discussion and feedback at the workshop.

* **Format**: Submissions use the ICLR template and contain 4–9 pages of main text, excluding references and appendices.
* **AI usage policy**: This track follows the ICLR 2027 AI Policy for Authors. Authors must disclose AI use as required by that policy, both in the submission form and in a dedicated disclosure section, which does not count towards the page limit. The human authors remain fully responsible for the submission, including its accuracy, originality, attribution, and the validity of any AI-produced claims or artifacts. Submissions that violate the disclosure or research-integrity requirements may be desk rejected.


## Tiny Paper Track

The Tiny Paper Track welcomes concise, self-contained contributions across the same scope. We particularly encourage late-breaking developments, focused results, and work from researchers who may be less familiar with the ML conference publication process. Suitable contributions include an implementation and evaluation of a simple idea, a modest theoretical result, a follow-up experiment or re-analysis of published work, or a fresh perspective on an existing publication.

* **Format**: Tiny papers use the ICLR template and are 1–2 pages of main text. They are evaluated according to their intended scope, without requiring the breadth of a full-length research paper.
* **AI usage policy**: AI-generated papers are not permitted in this track. AI tools may assist the authors, but submissions must remain primarily human-authored and present the authors' own scientific reasoning and analysis. Permitted AI assistance must be disclosed in accordance with the ICLR author policy. Submissions that do not meet these requirements will be desk rejected, and the organizing committee may request clarification of how AI tools were used.


## AI Research Track (Experimental)

Echoing the theme of this workshop, we do not regard AI-assisted or AI-generated scientific research as inherently undesirable. Understanding where AI involvement in research is useful, reliable, and scientifically meaningful is itself an important research question. This track is intended for submissions in which an AI system or research agent carries out a substantial part of the research process on a concrete problem in probabilistic inference or generative modeling — for example, proposing a method, deriving an objective or algorithm, implementing the approach, conducting experiments, analyzing results, or an end-to-end research workflow.

* **Authorship**: Submissions whose scientific content is substantially, or even primarily, generated by AI systems are explicitly permitted. However, the human authors remain fully responsible for the submission. Each author may be associated with **at most two** submissions to this track.
* **Scope**: Each submission must address a clearly specified problem in probabilistic inference or generative modeling. Conventional papers that use language models for coding, drafting, literature search, or other supporting tasks should be submitted to the Standard Research Track. Papers whose primary contribution is a general-purpose AI scientist or research-agent system are out of scope, unless they demonstrate that system by producing and independently validating a substantive scientific contribution to probabilistic inference or generative modeling.
* **Format**: Submissions use a separate submission template (to be released) and will be prominently labeled as AI Research Track submissions. Authors must document the AI system or agent used, the tasks delegated to it, the extent and nature of human intervention, and the procedure used to independently validate the resulting claims. Authors are also encouraged to include the prompts.
* **Review**: Each submission receives at least one human review and an independent review from an AI reviewing agent provided by the organizers. Acceptance decisions remain solely the responsibility of human reviewers and organizers. The parallel reviews let us study where human and agent assessments agree and differ.
* **Oral and prize**: One outstanding AI Research Track submission will be selected for an oral presentation and will receive a prize of either a three-month subscription to an AI research service (approximately USD 600 in value) or an equivalent Amazon gift card.


## Review Process

All three tracks are non-archival and managed through OpenReview, with submissions clearly identified by track. Each Standard Research Track and Tiny Paper Track submission will be assigned at least two human reviewers. Conflicts of interest are managed through OpenReview, and each submission is handled by a non-conflicted organizer and assigned to non-conflicted reviewers.

Accepted papers will be presented as posters. Six papers will be selected for oral presentations: one from the AI Research Track, and five from the Standard Research Track and the Tiny Paper Track.
